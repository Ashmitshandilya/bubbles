WHO Healthy Diet — Curated Evidence Summary

The World Health Organization states that healthy diets help prevent malnutrition and reduce risk associated with noncommunicable diseases. A healthy diet should be adequate, balanced, moderate and diverse, while also being safe.

The appropriate composition of a healthy diet varies with factors such as age, sex, lifestyle, physical activity, cultural context and locally available foods. Minimally processed and unprocessed foods can form the foundation of a healthy diet, with emphasis on diverse sources of vegetables, fruits, whole grains, pulses, nuts and appropriate protein sources.

WHO guidance recommends limiting free sugars and sodium, and limiting saturated and trans fats. For adults, WHO states that salt intake should be below 5 grams per day and free sugars should generally remain below 10% of total daily energy intake. The quality of dietary fat matters, with unsaturated fats generally preferred over saturated and trans fats.

WHO also emphasizes that nutrition needs differ between individuals and across life stages. Therefore, general dietary guidance should not automatically be treated as individualized medical or nutritional advice.
